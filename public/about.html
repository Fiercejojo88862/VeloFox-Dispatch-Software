Our story

We started because one driver was waiting in the wrong lot.

It was 11:40 at Newark, rain coming down sideways, and a passenger with two suitcases and no idea who was supposed to pick her up. Our first car spent forty minutes circling the wrong terminal. We have been fixing that particular mistake ever since.

New York · New Jersey · quietly at it since 2017

The early days

Luxury is mostly the boring things done right.

People remember the car, of course. But the calls we get are about the other stuff: the chauffeur who texted before the plane landed, the extra ten minutes while a child got settled into a car seat, the driver who knew to use the side entrance at the Peninsula because the lobby was closed for an event.

We have also learned to say the unglamorous things out loud. If a flight is delayed and the car is sitting in traffic on the Turnpike, you hear it from us before you notice. If we cannot make a pickup time, we say so and offer options. Clients forgive a delay much faster than they forgive being left guessing.

How we got here

A whiteboard, a notebook, and a lot of early flights.

2017

One sedan and a notebook

A garage in Jersey City, one black S-Class, and a handwritten list of flights we had learned to watch. The first months were mostly referrals and phone calls.

2019

A board on the wall

We put a whiteboard in the office and wrote every pickup on it. It was ugly, but for the first time everyone could see which car was due where — and who was running late.

2022

The board became software

Live assignments, GPS, quotes and receipts. We built it because we kept hitting the same gaps ourselves, not because the world needed another dashboard.

2026

Four classes, same habit

Sedan, SUV, Sprinter and stretch limousine now cover the tri-state area. The routine has not changed: check the flight, call before the client has to ask, leave room for the unexpected.

The people

The same chauffeurs, not whoever happens to be free.

Marcus has been doing airport pickups since 2011 and can tell you which arrivals hall at JFK moves faster on a Friday afternoon. Elena keeps ginger ale in the Sprinter because she once had a client who felt carsick on the way to a wedding. Neither of them reads from a script. Both will call you by name.

That is the whole idea. The car matters, obviously. But the person in the front seat is the reason anyone calls us a second time.

Still us

Still a small team, on purpose.

There is no call centre to navigate and no script to get through. You reach a dispatcher who can see the cars, the drivers and your booking on the same screen. If something goes sideways, you talk to someone who can actually fix it — usually the same person who answered.
